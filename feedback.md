## Detailed Iteration Notes

### Iteration 1 — Baseline Assessment
**Activity:** All three students took a 10-word vocabulary quiz (all lessons) and answered comprehension questions on "Those Winter Sundays."

- **Alex (9/10, 13/15):** Missed "concurrent" — confused it with "consecutive." Strong poetry analysis but didn't cite specific line numbers in answers.
- **Blake (7/10, 10/15):** Missed "ambiguous," "concurrent," "substantiate." Identified the poem's theme but couldn't explain how imagery supports it.
- **Casey (4/10, 6/15):** Only knew basic words (narrative, evidence, tone). Couldn't identify metaphor in the poem. Wrote "I don't know" for inference question.

**Feedback:** Casey needs scaffolding — definitions should show examples more prominently. Blake needs guided evidence-citing practice.

**Improvement Applied:** Added example sentences to flashcard back side; added vocabulary word highlighting in reading passages.

---

### Iteration 2 — Focused Review (Lessons 1-2)
**Activity:** Flashcard study of Lessons 1-2, then quiz. Reading: "Mother to Son."

- **Alex (9/10, 14/15):** Mastered all L1-2 words. Excellent analysis of extended metaphor ("crystal stair"). Missed nuance of "chronic" in context.
- **Blake (7/10, 11/15):** Improved on "ambiguous" after flashcard review. Identified simile but missed extended metaphor concept.
- **Casey (5/10, 7/15):** Flashcard mode helped — learned "convey" and "narrative." Still struggling with "infer" and "elaborate." Engaged more with the 3D flip animation.

**Feedback:** The gamification (XP, streaks) is keeping Casey engaged despite low scores. Blake benefits from the literary devices reference panel.

**Improvement Applied:** Added difficulty levels to vocabulary words (easy/medium/hard).

### Iteration 3 — Literary Terms Focus (Lessons 3-4)
**Activity:** Lesson 3-4 vocabulary quiz. Reading passage analysis with literary device identification.

- **Alex (10/10, 13/15):** Perfect vocab score. Identified all devices in poetry but rushed through historical passage.
- **Blake (8/10, 10/15):** Improved +1. Now understands metaphor vs. simile. Struggled with "cliché" vs "imagery" distinction.
- **Casey (5/10, 7/15):** Learned "metaphor" and "simile" through repeated flashcard exposure. Fill-in-the-blank too hard — typed "simmily" for simile.

**Feedback:** Casey needs case-insensitive matching for fill-in-the-blank. Spelling shouldn't block learning.

**Improvement Applied:** Made fill-in-the-blank case-insensitive; added hint system (-5 XP for first letter).

### Iteration 4 — Fill-in-the-Blank Introduction

**Feedback:** Fill-in-the-blank is effective for deeper learning but needs fuzzy matching for minor typos. The XP/level system is Casey's primary motivator.

## Final Recommendations

### For Maximizing Learning

1. **Spaced Repetition is Critical:** Words not reviewed within 3-4 sessions are forgotten. The "Review Missed Words" feature should be surfaced more prominently.

2. **Context Clues > Raw Memorization:** Teaching students HOW to figure out unknown words (context clues strategy) produces larger gains than memorizing more definitions.

3. **Vocabulary Highlighting in Passages:** This single feature produced the largest comprehension gains for mid-range students (Blake and Casey). It bridges the gap between vocabulary study and reading comprehension.

4. **AI Chat for Higher-Order Skills:** Inference, analysis, and evidence-citing are best practiced through dialogue with the AI tutor, not through quizzes.

5. **Gamification Drives Engagement for Lower-Performing Students:** Casey's primary motivator was XP and leveling up. Without gamification, Casey would likely have disengaged after iteration 3.

6. **Difficulty Adaptation Matters:** Alex needed harder content by iteration 5. Casey needed easier content until iteration 8. One-size-fits-all doesn't work.

### Recommended Additions

- **Synonym/Antonym Quiz Mode:** Dedicated mode for matching synonyms and antonyms
- **Sentence Writing Practice:** Have students write original sentences using vocab words, graded by AI
- **Passage Difficulty Progression:** Unlock harder passages as easier ones are mastered
- **Weekly Progress Report:** Email/display showing improvement over time
- **Practice Test Mode:** Full timed standardized test simulation with mixed question types
- **Audio Pronunciation:** Hearing words spoken aids retention

### Key Finding

> **The largest percentile gains came from Casey (50th → 72nd, +22 points).** This suggests the site is most impactful for students who need the most help — the gamification keeps them engaged, the scaffolding builds confidence, and the AI feedback provides personalized guidance they wouldn't get from a textbook.
